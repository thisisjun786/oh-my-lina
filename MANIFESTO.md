# LINA Manifesto

**LINA - Lifelong Intelligent Navigator & Ally.**

**Your world. One connected mind.**

A personal agent that carries conversation, experience, and work through to verified outcomes.

We’re building LINA to be an ally who knows you and a personal agent you can count on. One LINA to think aloud with, share a ridiculous idea with, and trust to get things done. A conversation is worth having even when it produces no deliverable.

We gave computers brains. Then hired ourselves as their clipboard.

Copy this into another session. Explain the project again. Pick a folder. Nudge the agent. Approve the next obvious step. Repeat until the future feels suspiciously like middle management.

We would like our species reassigned.

## One conversation. Room for a life.

Over time, LINA learns your world: what matters to you, what you have tried, and what you are still figuring out. That shared history travels with you across devices and years. New machine. Same LINA. No reintroductions.

Keep talking. Change subjects. Start something else while the first thing runs.

LINA carries the context and keeps the initiatives moving in the background. Progress and results return to the same conversation. The machinery can have a thousand sessions. You should never have to carry a message between them.

## Bring the problem

Start with something you want to solve. One sentence is enough to begin.

Talk it through with LINA. Find the thing worth doing together. That becomes an initiative: a purpose LINA can turn into work.

The need can surface halfway through a joke, a complaint, or an unrelated conversation. LINA remembers those loose ends, notices what keeps coming up, and brings back useful suggestions. A passing thought should not need a project kickoff ceremony to survive.

## The legwork belongs to the machine

LINA turns an initiative into projects and issues, does the deep research, and orchestrates the agents that will do the work. Each project has a parent agent coordinating its issue agents. LINA keeps the whole effort pointed at the original problem.

Planning, research, execution, verification. LINA owns all of it. Routine work moves forward without asking a human to approve the obvious. You decide what’s worth solving and whether the result actually solves it.

A beautiful plan with nothing working at the end is still nothing working at the end.

## The work outlives the workspace

A folder on one computer is a terrible place to anchor a purpose.

Where the work happens is LINA’s problem. Decisions, changes, and results must endure, with versions you can inspect and restore. GitHub commits are one way. The principle applies just as much to research, documents, and everything else worth doing.

Change the machine. Keep the work.

## Build the strange thing. Make it work.

We build with OMO and Senpi, carrying forward OMO's [ultrawork philosophy](https://github.com/code-yeongyu/oh-my-openagent/blob/dev/docs/manifesto.md): take ownership of the work, spare the human the babysitting, and prove it is done.

Give LINA memory. Let experience shape who it becomes. Try the strange idea. Keep a lab notebook. Keep what works.

Humanity has spent enough time implementing the clipboard interface.

> **You provide intent. The agent handles everything.**

With respect for [Oh My OpenAgent](https://github.com/code-yeongyu/oh-my-openagent) and the people building it.
